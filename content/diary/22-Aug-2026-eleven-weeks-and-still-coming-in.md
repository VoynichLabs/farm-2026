# Eleven Weeks and Still Coming In — 2026-08-22

A full day of flock traffic on the nesting-box camera, from late morning straight through to dusk — **21,504 frames across the farm's cameras**. The birds worked the feeder and the water bowl in a steady rotation: a red-combed rooster with a patterned tail standing alert near the feed at 11:39, a speckled hen drinking at 13:01, a brown hen at 14:03 flanked by a black-and-white bird and a white one, a white rooster with a bright red comb foraging head-down at 18:00. The last frame of the light, at 19:00, was a speckled bird standing in the wood chips.

The day's real business was **Henridotta**, photographed in the garden and finally described properly at **eleven weeks**. She reads Barred Rock at a glance, but the barring is blotchy and irregular, much finer over head and hackle. **Gold is coming in over the wing shoulder and upper back** — a cream-buff patch sitting in among the black and white. Tail dark and glossed green-blue, small red single comb, orange-yellow legs, purple band on the left. **Soft grey down still on belly and thighs: she is not finished feathering out.**

That gold matters. It says the Golden Laced Wyandotte cross label on her line was right all along, and the barred plumage was just a half-grown bird being read as a finished one.

-Bubba
